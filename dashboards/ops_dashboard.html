Needs attention now Every figure below is computed from the current snapshot — not opinion.

1

Concentrate hardening effort on Delivery Pipeline

Raw bug counts barely separate the modules, but size does: Delivery Pipeline packs 8,364 defects into only 31.7 KLOC — 263.8 per KLOC, 4.4x the density of Core Services (59.5 per KLOC over 142.5 KLOC), with 6,044 still open. Per line of code touched, a refactor and test-coverage push there removes more defect mass than the same effort spread across all six modules.

2

P3 is the weakest link in the SLA

Only 70.8% of closed P3 bugs met their 14-day target (average 11.9 days, median 10.0). Overall SLA compliance is 77.6%. Either the P3 target is unrealistic for the work it covers, or triage is under-staffing that queue — re-baseline the target or reserve sprint capacity for it.

3

Clear the aged backlog before it becomes technical debt

33,528 of the 35,852 open bugs (93.5%) have already passed their SLA target, and the average open bug is 182.4 days old. Run an explicit aging sweep each sprint: close, defer with a reason, or escalate — bugs that age silently distort every downstream estimate.

4

Intake outruns closure — cap work-in-progress

The backlog grew in 26 of 26 sprints and ends at 35,852 open bugs. SPR-01 was the worst (1,989 opened vs 208 closed). Holding a fixed closure quota per sprint — rather than a fixed intake — is the only way this curve flattens.

5

Three root causes drive a third of all defects

'Misconfiguration or logic issue related to memory leak' alone accounts for 3,220 bugs (6.4%), and the top three causes together account for 19.2%. These are systemic, not incidental: a single guard rail (lint rule, contract test, review checklist) against each removes recurring work from every future sprint.

6

Release v2.2 carries the largest urgent queue

v2.2 still has 1,450 unresolved P1/P2 bugs, out of 4,133 open and 5,723 total. Closure rates sit near 27.8% across every release, so the differentiator is volume, not discipline. Gate the next release on the urgent queue being drained rather than on the calendar.

7

The routing policy concentrates a quarter of all work on one role

Backend Developer is routed 13,004 bugs (26.0% of everything) and is sitting on 3,145 open P1/P2, while Data Engineer receives 2,586. Turnaround is nearly identical across all 7 roles (15.7–17.2 days), so the queue length — not individual speed — is what delays urgent fixes. Re-route one or two bug categories, or add capacity where the policy points.
